HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE)

Overview
Hypertension is a chronic medical condition in which the force of blood against the walls of arteries is consistently too high. Over time, uncontrolled hypertension can damage blood vessels and vital organs such as the heart, brain, kidneys, and eyes.

Blood Pressure Physiology
Blood pressure is measured using two numbers:
Systolic pressure – pressure during heart contraction
Diastolic pressure – pressure when the heart relaxes

Normal blood pressure: 120/80 mmHg

Types
Primary Hypertension
Develops gradually over many years and has no identifiable cause.

Secondary Hypertension
Caused by underlying conditions such as kidney disease, hormonal disorders, or medications.

Symptoms
Often called the “silent killer” because many people have no symptoms.
Possible symptoms include:
Headache
Shortness of breath
Nosebleeds
Chest pain
Fatigue

Risk Factors
Age
Family history
Obesity
High salt intake
Smoking
Alcohol consumption
Stress

Diagnosis
Blood pressure measurement
Blood tests
Urine tests
Electrocardiogram

Treatment
Lifestyle changes
Blood pressure medications
Reduced salt diet
Regular exercise

Prevention
Healthy diet
Regular physical activity
Weight management
Stress reduction
Avoid smoking
