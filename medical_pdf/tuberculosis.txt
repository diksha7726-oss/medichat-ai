TUBERCULOSIS (TB)

Overview
Tuberculosis is a contagious infectious disease caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It primarily affects the lungs but can also affect other parts of the body such as the brain, kidneys, and spine. Tuberculosis spreads through the air when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or speaks.

History and Global Impact
Tuberculosis has affected humans for thousands of years and remains a major global health concern. According to global health organizations, millions of new TB cases occur every year, particularly in developing countries where access to healthcare may be limited.

Types of Tuberculosis
Latent Tuberculosis
In latent TB infection, the bacteria remain in the body in an inactive state. Individuals do not show symptoms and cannot spread the disease, but the infection can become active later.

Active Tuberculosis
Active TB occurs when the bacteria multiply and cause symptoms. It can spread from person to person through airborne particles.

Symptoms
Persistent cough lasting more than three weeks
Chest pain
Coughing up blood
Fatigue
Weight loss
Fever
Night sweats
Loss of appetite

Causes
Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacteria. The infection spreads through airborne droplets released when an infected person coughs or sneezes.

Risk Factors
Weak immune system
HIV infection
Malnutrition
Diabetes
Living in crowded conditions
Smoking
Alcohol abuse

Diagnosis
Tuberculin skin test
Blood tests
Chest X-ray
Sputum test
Molecular diagnostic tests

Treatment
Treatment typically involves a combination of antibiotics taken for several months. Common medications include isoniazid, rifampicin, ethambutol, and pyrazinamide.

Complications
Untreated TB can lead to severe lung damage, meningitis, kidney damage, and spinal problems.

Prevention
Vaccination with the BCG vaccine
Early detection and treatment
Improved ventilation in crowded areas
Proper cough hygiene
Regular medical screening in high-risk populations
