MALARIA

Overview
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites that are transmitted to humans through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. It is common in tropical and subtropical regions.

Parasite Life Cycle
The malaria parasite enters the bloodstream through a mosquito bite and travels to the liver where it multiplies. It then infects red blood cells, leading to the symptoms of malaria.

Types of Malaria Parasites
Plasmodium falciparum
Plasmodium vivax
Plasmodium malariae
Plasmodium ovale
Plasmodium knowlesi

Symptoms
High fever
Chills
Sweating
Headache
Muscle pain
Fatigue
Nausea and vomiting

Severe Symptoms
Severe anemia
Organ failure
Seizures
Coma

Risk Factors
Living in malaria-endemic regions
Lack of mosquito protection
Poor sanitation
Travel to tropical regions

Diagnosis
Blood smear microscopy
Rapid diagnostic tests
Molecular diagnostic tests

Treatment
Antimalarial medications such as artemisinin-based combination therapies
Supportive care
Hospital treatment for severe cases

Prevention
Using insecticide-treated bed nets
Indoor residual spraying
Mosquito control programs
Preventive medications for travelers

Public Health Importance
Malaria remains a major global health problem, particularly in Africa and parts of Asia. Efforts to control malaria include improved healthcare access, mosquito control, and vaccine development.
